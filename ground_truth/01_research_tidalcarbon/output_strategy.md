# Strategic Communication & Engagement Plan
### TidalCarbon (Research Project Team)

## 1. Executive Summary
TidalCarbon holds a genuinely fundable, policy-relevant result — a 40% improvement in blue-carbon sequestration from a low-cost sediment-management technique — but no comms capacity to convert it into follow-on funding before the project closes. With ~£5,000, no dedicated comms staff and the end-of-project policy workshop (month 30) as a natural flagship moment, the priority is concentration, not reach: a single evidence-led funder-and-policy narrative, delivered through targeted 1:1 briefings and the workshop, backed by the credibility of two peer-reviewed papers and an open dataset. This plan trades broad public engagement for depth with the ~20–30 people who control follow-on funding and coastal policy.

## 2. Stakeholder Analysis

| Stakeholder | Interest | Influence | Engagement Approach |
|---|---|---|---|
| Research funders (EPSRC/NERC, impact & follow-on funds) | Continuation of demonstrable impact, value for money, scalability | High | Two-page concept note + impact one-pager; personal invite to the workshop; early informal "future funding" conversations |
| Coastal policymakers (Defra, Environment Agency, LA climate leads) | Evidence for net-zero and coastal-resilience targets | High | Plain-English policy brief; 1:1 briefings; the policy workshop as the anchor event |
| Environmental NGOs (Wildlife Trusts, WWF, RSPB, Blue Marine) | Restoration outcomes, credible science to support advocacy | Med–High | Partnership meetings; offer co-branded summary; invite as workshop co-host |
| Coastal local authorities | Local climate resilience, access to restoration funding | Medium | Site visit / short briefing; plain-English summary tied to their coastline |
| Academic peers & potential co-investigators | Citation, replication, collaboration on a follow-on bid | Medium | Two journal papers + open dataset; one targeted conference; preprint |
| Local communities & regional media | Local benefit, a good environmental story | Low–Med | One explainer + one regional press release timed to the workshop |

## 3. Key Messages
**A simple change to sediment management lifts coastal carbon capture by 40%.** TidalCarbon's technique is low-cost and replicable, turning existing wetlands into measurably stronger carbon sinks.

**Blue carbon is shovel-ready climate infrastructure for the UK coast.** This is a method that can be deployed now to help meet net-zero and coastal-resilience targets, not a distant lab prospect.

**The evidence is open, peer-reviewed and replicable.** Two journal papers and a public dataset mean funders and policymakers can trust — and others can verify — the 40% result.

**A modest follow-on investment scales a validated method.** The hard de-risking is done; the next round is about moving from proof to deployment at coastal scale.

## 4. Communication Channels
- **Direct 1:1 funder and policymaker briefings** — highest conversion for a funding/policy goal, near-zero cost; the single most important channel.
- **The month-30 policy workshop (flagship event)** — anchor all activity to it; it concentrates the right people in one room and justifies the budget.
- **Designed policy brief + impact one-pager (PDF)** — ~£1,000 of the £5k on professional design; the core leave-behind for every briefing.
- **LinkedIn (PI + project account)** — free, and reaches funders, policy and NGO professionals directly; a light, consistent cadence around milestones.
- **University press office + one regional media piece** — credibility and reach at no direct cost; the press office handles distribution.
- **Open dataset + a simple project page** — a durable, citable credibility asset that keeps working after the project ends.
- *(De-prioritised: paid ads, broad social campaigns, multiple conferences — poor fit for a £5k budget and a funder/policy audience.)*

## 6. Risk Assessment

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| No comms capacity / researcher time runs out | High | High | Name one comms owner with ~2 hrs/week; pre-package reusable assets; favour 1:1s over broadcast |
| Headline "40%" over-claimed before peer review | Medium | High | Gate bold claims behind accepted papers; always state the caveat and conditions; quote the figure with its confidence range |
| Low policymaker/funder turnout at the workshop | Medium | High | Secure 2–3 anchor attendees and an NGO co-host early; book dates 6+ months ahead |
| Follow-on funding window missed | Medium | High | Start concept notes by month 6 — not after the workshop; track relevant calls from the outset |

## 7. Next Steps
1. **Appoint one comms owner** on the team and protect ~2 hours/week for the next 12 months.
2. **Finalise the three key messages** and agree the exact caveats on the "40%" figure.
3. **Build the 30-name target list** of funders, policymakers, NGOs and local authorities.
4. **Commission the policy brief + one-pager design** (~£1,000 of the budget).
5. **Book five warm 1:1 briefings** in the next six weeks and confirm the workshop date with an NGO co-host.
